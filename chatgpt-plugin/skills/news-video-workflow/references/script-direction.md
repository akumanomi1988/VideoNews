# Script direction: specific, spoken and useful

Write the explanation the audience came for. Do not fill runtime by narrating our
editorial method or repeating that an investigation is incomplete. State a limit
once where it affects a claim; repeat it only when a genuinely different claim
could otherwise mislead. Attribution and caution should support the story.

Before drafting, make a compact evidence outline: the video's central question,
the supported answer so far, three to five concrete facts, relevant context,
an explanatory example and a consequence. If sources cannot support these,
research further instead of substituting vague questions and rhetorical filler.

Use a fact/example/consequence progression for each section. Each paragraph must
add something the preceding paragraph did not provide. Include specific verified
dates, actors, mechanisms or measurements when relevant. Compare quantities only
with a valid baseline. Label analogies and interpretation; never invent a quote,
exclusive detail, victim story or causal link.

Open in the first ten seconds with a specific stake or contrast. By thirty seconds,
the viewer should know the question and why it matters. Deliver information
throughout instead of withholding the answer until the end. Close with the answer
and its implications, not another recitation of the full script. One short CTA is
optional; omit it when inappropriate, particularly for tragedies.

Write Spanish from Spain for a person speaking: active verbs, familiar connectors,
one idea per sentence and varied sentence lengths. Most sentences can be around
8-20 words, with longer ones where an explanation requires it. Use full stops and
paragraph breaks for natural breathing. Avoid academic introductions, bureaucratic
noun chains, identical openings and stacked rhetorical questions. Do not use SSML,
emotion tags, stage directions or repeated ellipses to manufacture expression.
Handle abbreviations, names and numbers for clear pronunciation without changing
their meaning. Check uncertain pronunciations instead of guessing.

Read the draft aloud if possible. Remove repeated caveats, meta-comments about
"this video", empty transitions and unsupported adjectives. Voice quality also
depends on speakable writing; a new TTS model cannot fix a monotonous script.
Keep the current 3-5 minute brief and verify actual synthesized duration.

## Concrete revision example

Weak: "A record is one piece of the investigation, not a verdict. There are open
questions. We need more information. In NewSphere we will keep checking sources."

Better structure: state exactly what the source says the record establishes,
explain what it does not establish once, and move to the next supported fact or
mechanism. Do not invent the record's contents to make this example concrete.
When no additional fact is available, shorten the section instead of paraphrasing
the same uncertainty several times.
